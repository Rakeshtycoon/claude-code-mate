## 1. What diamond symmetry is

Symmetry = how precisely a diamond's facets are aligned and how exact its proportions are.
It is part of the **cut** grade (the "C" that most affects how a diamond reflects light) and
is assessed under **10× magnification**.

GIA's formal definition: **"the exactness of the shape and placement of a diamond's facets."**

## 2. The grading scale

**Excellent → Very Good → Good → Fair → Poor**

| Grade | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| Excellent | Facets align essentially perfectly; no misalignment visible at 10× |
| Very Good | Minor misalignments with negligible effect on appearance |
| Good | More noticeable misalignment; can slightly reduce brilliance |
| Fair | Visible misalignment that distorts light reflection |
| Poor | Significant misalignment clearly affecting appearance |

## 3. Common symmetry flaws

- **Off-center culet** (bottom point not centered)
- **Off-center / misshapen table** (flat top facet)
- **Misaligned facets** (facets that don't meet cleanly — crown vs pavilion at the girdle)
- **Crown / pavilion angle variation** (the eight angles not equal)
- **Wavy / uneven girdle** (the band around the widest part)
- **Girdle thickness variation**
- **Extra facets** (usually near the girdle, often added to remove an inclusion)
- **Missing or misshapen facets**
- **Naturals** (leftover rough "skin" that can make the girdle out-of-round)

## 4. Why symmetry matters / value impact

- Better symmetry → light enters and exits more efficiently → more **brightness and fire**.
- Excellent vs Very Good polish+symmetry ≈ **10–12% price difference**, but the two are
  visually indistinguishable to the naked eye.
- **Buying rule of thumb:** Round brilliants → Very Good or Excellent. Fancy shapes →
  Good or better. Never go below Good. **Very Good is the value sweet spot** — spend the
  saved money on cut proportions and color/clarity you can actually see.
- Cutters often prioritise **carat weight over perfect symmetry** (a 1.00 ct "Good symmetry"
  stone is more valuable/marketable than a 0.96 ct "Excellent symmetry" stone from the same rough).

## 5. Symmetry vs polish vs cut grade

- **Polish** and **symmetry** are graded separately; both make up a diamond's *finish*.
- The **cut grade** (round brilliant) has 7 components: brightness, fire, scintillation,
  weight ratio, durability, **polish**, **symmetry**.
- A diamond can earn **Excellent cut** with **Very Good** symmetry if its proportions and
  light performance are excellent — symmetry is one input, not the whole story.

## 6. Light performance — why a diamond sparkles

Four terms describe the optical show:

1. **Brilliance** — overall *white* brightness. External reflection (off the surface) +
   internal reflection (light entering, bouncing off pavilion facets, exiting the top).
2. **Fire (dispersion)** — the *rainbow* flashes. Diamond's high refractive index splits
   white light into spectral colors (prism effect).
3. **Scintillation** — the *sparkle/twinkle* seen in motion; the pattern of light and dark
   as the diamond, light, or viewer moves.
4. **Dispersion** — the physical cause of fire (splitting of white light into colors).

**How cut controls it:** light enters the table/crown, should totally internally reflect off
the pavilion, and exit back up. Ideal proportions → most light returns → bright, lively stone.
Too shallow or too deep → light "leaks" out the bottom/sides → dull stone. Symmetry and polish
fine-tune the crispness of the flashes.

**Proportions sweet spot (round brilliant):**

| Parameter | Range |
|-----------|-------|
| Table % | 54–58% |
| Total depth % | 60–62.3% |
| Crown angle | 34–35° |
| Pavilion angle | 40.6–40.9° |

- **Tolkowsky ideal (1919):** crown 34.5°, pavilion 40.75°, table 53%, depth 59.3%.
- **Fire ↔ brilliance trade-off:** steeper crown = more fire; shallower crown = more white
  brilliance. Lighting matters: spot/direct light favors fire (pavilion angle dominant);
  diffuse/ambient light favors brilliance (crown angle dominant).

## 7. Fluorescence (a related 4Cs factor)

- **Fluorescence** = a natural (usually blue) glow some diamonds emit under **UV light**.
  Graded **None → Faint → Medium → Strong → Very Strong**. ~25–35% of diamonds show some.
- **Helpful on lower colors (H and below):** blue glow offsets yellow tint → looks whiter.
- **Potentially harmful on high colors (D–G):** strong fluorescence can occasionally cause a
  hazy/"oily" look (a minority of stones).
- **Value:** fluorescent stones can sell up to ~15% less. For D–F any fluorescence nudges
  price down; for H–K, Medium/Strong can be a smart, cheaper route to a whiter look.
- Usually invisible to the naked eye indoors — always view the specific stone (ideally in sunlight).

## 8. GIA official symmetry data (summary)

- Definition: "the exactness of the shape and placement of a diamond's facets." Graded at 10×.
- **10 measurable symmetry parameters** (Geurts et al., Gems & Gemology, Winter 2011; used to
  support/constrain the visual grade since 2012):
  1. Out-of-round
  2. Table size variation
  3. Crown angle variation
  4. Crown height variation
  5. Pavilion angle variation
  6. Girdle thickness variation
  7. Table off-center
  8. Culet off-center
  9. Table/culet alignment
  10. Pavilion-to-crown (table-to-culet) misalignment
- A "variation" = spread (max − min) among like facets; the **worst single parameter caps the grade**.
- Two kinds of symmetry: **proportion / meet-point** (graded by GIA) vs **optical / index
  symmetry** (drives Hearts & Arrows; *not* graded by GIA).
