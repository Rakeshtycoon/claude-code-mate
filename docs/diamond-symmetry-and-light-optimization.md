# Diamond Facet-Angle Symmetry & Light Optimization — Deep Analysis

> Research compiled from multi-source web research (GIA, AGS, PriceScope, OctoNus,
> Sarine and gemological references). Most primary sites block automated fetching
> (HTTP 403), so claims are cross-checked against WebSearch snippets of those same
> authoritative pages plus accessible mirrors. Uncertainties are flagged inline.

## 0. The core concept (verified)

A diamond's reported facet angles are **averages**, while individual facets can vary —
and cutters deliberately exploit this to retain weight and/or tune light return.

| Claim | Accuracy | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| Pavilion shows as 40° but individual facets are 39, 36, 41, 42… averaging ~40 | ✅ True | The report's "Pavilion Angle" is the **mean of the 8 pavilion-main facets** (to 0.1°). |
| Average is kept the same while symmetry is changed | ⚠️ Half-true | The cutter shifts individual facets (technique = **painting/digging**); the average stays in the ideal range, so the certificate/symmetry grade still looks good. |
| Symmetry is tuned so the diamond reflects maximum light = maximum sparkle | ✅ True | This is **light-performance optimization**, done with ray-tracing software (DiamCalc, Sarine Light, AGS ASET). |

**Caveat:** "keeping the average the same on purpose" is not a grading rule — it is cutter
*manipulation* that takes advantage of the averaging loophole. Also, perfect symmetry ≠
perfect light performance; they are distinct contributors.

## 1. Averaging — how a "single angle" appears on a report

- The reported **Crown Angle** and **Pavilion Angle** are the **mean of the 8 main facets**, to 0.1°. (gia.edu cut booklet; pricescope)
- In low-symmetry stones these 8 values can differ by several degrees, yet collapse to one
  average — which is why a GIA report alone "does not tell you how precisely the diamond was
  cut." (beyond4cs; whiteflash)
- The 3D data is captured by **non-contact optical scanners** (Sarine/Sarin, OGI): ~400 profile
  images around 360°, reassembled into a 3D model, each facet's angle + azimuth to ~20-micron
  accuracy, then averaged. (beyond4cs; sarine.com; ogisystems)
- The **cut grade** is also computed on averages (Facetware lookup); individual asymmetry is
  tolerated as long as averages land in the grade's range. (gia facetware-help)

> Individual-facet variation is captured in exactly one place — the **Symmetry grade**
> (and, since 2012, GIA's 10 symmetry parameters). Everywhere else, averages rule.

## 2. Two kinds of symmetry

| | Meet-point symmetry | Optical / "index" symmetry |
|---|---|---|
| What | Do facet junction lines meet at correct single points? | Are facet size/shape/**azimuth indexing** consistent 360° around? |
| Defect | "Pointing fault", "twist" | "**Yaw**" = azimuthal twisting of facets |
| GIA grades it? | ✅ Yes — this *is* the "Symmetry" grade | ❌ No — not graded by GIA |
| Drives | Certificate symmetry grade | Hearts & Arrows pattern, sparkle uniformity |

Sources: yourdiamondteacher; goodoldgold; victorcanera; beyond4cs.

> A stone can be GIA "Excellent" symmetry yet still have indexing errors, facet tilt, or large
> angle variation. "Excellent symmetry" ≠ optical perfection.

## 3. GIA's 10 measurable symmetry parameters

Paper: *Geurts, Reinitz, Blodgett & Gilbertson, "GIA's Symmetry Grading Boundaries for Round
Brilliant Cut Diamonds," Gems & Gemology, Winter 2011, Vol. 47(4), pp. 286–295.* Since early
2012 these numeric limits support/constrain the visual symmetry grade.

1. Out-of-round
2. Table size variation
3. **Crown angle variation (CV)**
4. Crown height variation
5. **Pavilion angle variation (PV)**
6. Girdle thickness variation
7. Table off-center
8. Culet off-center
9. Table/culet alignment
10. Pavilion-to-crown (table-to-culet) misalignment

- A "variation" = spread (max − min) among like facets (e.g. the 8 pavilion angles).
- Each parameter has numeric boundaries mapping to Excellent/VG/Good/Fair/Poor; the **worst
  single parameter caps the grade**.
- Example from the paper: out-of-round 0.7%, crown variation 1.1°, girdle variation 1.1% drops
  below Excellent — so the Excellent boundary sits near ~0.7–1.1% / ~1.1°. (Full Table 1 is in
  the gated GIA PDF / Semantic Scholar Table 1.)

## 4. "Painting & Digging" — the actual name of this technique

This is precisely the practice of keeping the **main angles' average in the ideal range while
shifting the small girdle facets**.

**Definition:** purposeful, symmetric displacement of the upper/lower girdle (halve) facets by
changing the index-wheel (azimuth) position when cutting. These halve facets cover ~40% of the
round brilliant's surface and lie on many light paths. (GIA via pricescope)

- **Painting** = halve facet tilted *toward* the adjacent main (angle decreases, blurs into the
  main). Index 1.
- **Digging out** = opposite — halve facet tilted *away* from the main. Index 3.
- **Normal / classic** = Index 2. (gemsociety; gemgate)

**Why cutters do it:**
1. **Weight retention** (primary) — save carat weight from the rough without changing the
   proportions on the certificate (e.g. to clear a magic weight like 1.00 ct). (prosumerdiamonds)
2. **Light improvement** — light upper-girdle painting brings angles closer to the mains →
   *less leakage*, larger "broadfire" scintillation. (Digging is always harmful.) (pricescope; jannpaul)

**Why it hides from averaged grades:**
> "Painting and digging out may not affect the symmetry grade, but it can affect the light
> performance. Therefore, it is possible to receive an Excellent symmetry grade but not an
> AGS Ideal 0 Light Performance grade." (jogiadiamonds; pricescope)

Because the main angles are averaged, shifting the small halve facets leaves the averages and
symmetry grade looking fine, but changes real light return. AGS built ASET/ray-traced detection
specifically to catch this; GIA folds it in as a "brillianteering of the half facets" factor
graded Negligible / Moderate / Significant / Severe. (jogiadiamonds; gemsociety; prosumerdiamonds)

**Detection:** ASET/Ideal-Scope reveals severity — digging shows a **green semicircle of leakage**
at the girdle; severe painting shows a washed-out, low-contrast pattern. (jannpaul; beyond4cs)

## 5. How light is maximized — optics + proportions

- **Tolkowsky ideal (1919):** crown 34.5°, pavilion 40.75°, table 53%, depth 59.3%. (nationaldiamond; niceice)
- **Modern sweet spot:** crown **34–35°**, pavilion **40.6–40.9°**, table **54–57%**. (beyond4cs)
- **Complementary pairing (key):** steeper crown ↔ shallower pavilion (~40.6°); steeper pavilion
  (~40.9°) ↔ shallower crown (~34°). Keeps light returning to the eye rather than leaking. (pricescope)
- **Fire ↔ brilliance trade-off:** steeper crown = more fire; shallower crown = more white-light
  return. They pull opposite ways and must be balanced. (whiteflash)
- **Star & lower-half lengths** (often ignored) set scintillation character and measurably shift
  WLR/DCLR. (pricescope; gia G&G 2001)
- **Asymmetry → leakage:** facet size/alignment/indexing differences create "obstruction" (stray
  reflections) that scatter/block returning light. (niceice; goodoldgold)

**GIA optical research metrics:**
- **WLR (Weighted Light Return)** — GIA 1998, 20,122 proportion combos ray-traced for brilliance.
  Finding: the optimum is *not a single point* — many crown/table combos match or beat "ideal" as
  pavilion → ~41°. (gia G&G 1998)
- **DCLR (Dispersed Colored Light Return)** — GIA 2001, fire metric, 26,000+ combos under directed
  light. WLR and DCLR peak at *different* proportions; designers seek overlap zones. (gia G&G 2001)

## 6. Automatic tools

### A) Measurement / scanning (build the 3D model)
| Tool | Function |
|---|---|
| **Sarine DiaScan / DiaMension** | Non-contact laser+photo; measures every facet ~20µm; proportions + symmetry (naturals, misalignment, junctions, extra facets) |
| **DiaMension AXIOM** | Micron-level (GIA-verified); facet pointing/junction/misalignment/distortion; only scanner enabling fully automated Cut & Symmetry grading |
| **OGI CutGrade** | Facet capture → angles mapped to GIA/AGS/IGI/HRD standards |
| **OctoNus Helium Rough / HP Oxygen** | Dual-head laser; rough + inclusion scan; "Reflect" per-facet illumination for accuracy |

### B) Optimization / design (tune facet angles for light/weight)
| Tool | Function |
|---|---|
| **OctoNus DiamCalc** | Individual facet edit (slope + azimuth); ray-tracing; Pro generates optimization charts across proportion ranges; virtual ASET/Ideal-Scope/H&A |
| **OctoNus Pacor** | Rough-allocation: most-profitable marking |
| **Sarine Advisor (8.0)** | World-standard rough planning; integrates Sarine Light to target a light grade = weight-vs-light trade-off |
| **Sarine Galaxy / DiaExpert** | Inclusion mapping + rough 3D model |

### C) Light-performance grading / verification
| Tool | Function |
|---|---|
| **Sarine Light** | Brilliance, Sparkle, Fire, Light Symmetry → overall Light Performance grade |
| **AGS Light Performance (now via GIA)** | 3D scan → ray-trace → ASET map; grades measured light behavior (0 = Ideal). AGSL closed 2022; GIA took IP, issues "AGS Ideal Report" supplement since Jan 2023 |
| **GIA Facetware** | 38.5M+ proportion sets lookup; averaged proportions → predicted cut grade (not official) |
| **HCA (Holloway Cut Adviser)** | 5 inputs → Light return/Fire/Scintillation/Spread; 0–10 (sub-2 = Excellent); rejection tool, not selection |

**Hierarchy:** HCA (proportion rejection) → Ideal-Scope (visual leakage) → ASET (angle-resolved
color) → full 3D ray-traced grading (GIA WLR/DCLR research; AGS/Sarine Light performance grade).

## 7. Bottom line

1. The reported pavilion 40° is the **average of 8 facets**; individuals can differ by degrees. ✅
2. Cutters shift individual halve facets (**painting/digging**) to keep the average ideal, so the
   certificate and symmetry grade still look good. ✅
3. This is done to **retain weight** and/or **tune light**. ✅
4. To **maximize light**, facet angles are optimized with ray-tracing tools (**DiamCalc, Sarine
   Light/Advisor, AGS ASET**); averaged GIA grading can miss the subtlety, which is why
   performance-based systems (AGS/Sarine) exist. ✅
