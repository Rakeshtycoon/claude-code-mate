> Britannica, Ganoksin, US Faceters Guild, PriceScope, GIA/AGS, OctoNus, Sarine, Synova,
> USPTO/Google Patents). Many primary sites block automated fetching (HTTP 403), so claims
> are cross-checked against WebSearch snippets and accessible mirrors. Uncertainties flagged inline.

## The core idea

Changing a diamond's symmetry means controlling **two parameters per facet**:
- **Slope (angle/depth)** — how steep the facet is.
- **Azimuth (index/rotation)** — which way the facet faces around the stone.

These two "knobs" create, refine, and manipulate all symmetry.

## 1. Which cutting stage establishes symmetry

| Stage | What happens | Effect on symmetry |
|---|---|---|
| **Planning / marking** | 3D laser scan (Sarine Galaxy/Advisor); laser guidelines on rough | *Intended* symmetry decided before any material is removed |
| **Cleaving / sawing** | Split the rough into pieces (often laser today) | Block of material, not the outline |
| **Bruting (girdling)** | Two diamonds ground against each other to form the round girdle | **Round/radial symmetry first physically created** (machine bruting since ~1870s made symmetric rounds possible) |
| **Blocking** | Blocker places first ~17–18 facets (8 pavilion mains, 8 crown mains, table, culet) | **Foundation of 8-fold symmetry** — table/culet centering + 8 main angles. "Sets the basic symmetry." |
| **Brillianteering** | Brillianteer adds the remaining ~40 facets (8 stars, 16 upper + 16 lower girdle halves) | Symmetry **refined** + fire added. Painting/digging happens here |
| **Final polish** | Final finish on the scaife | Polish (a separate grade) |

> Symmetry is **created at blocking and refined at brillianteering** — a two-person, two-stage
> outcome. "The first side's mains must sit exactly above the second side's mains; any fault here
> lowers commercial value."

## 2. How facet angle + azimuth are mechanically set

- The stone is cemented to a **dop** held in the machine's quill.
- **Angle (slope)** is set on the **mast / protractor** (0–90°; 90° = girdle, ~42° = pavilion main).
- **Azimuth (rotation)** is set on the **index wheel / gear**. A standard **96-tooth index** =
  360°/96 = **3.75° per step** (96 is divisible by 2,3,4,6,8,12,16,24,32,48).
- **N-fold symmetry = step by 96/N teeth.** For 8-fold, index every 12th tooth → 8 pavilion mains
  at index 6, 18, 30, 42, 54, 66, 78, 90 — evenly spaced.
- **Cutting depth** (a third variable) is set by lowering the quill until the facet reaches a
  **meet point**.
- **Meet-point cutting** (modern method): set angle + index, then cut until the new facet *just*
  reaches an existing junction. Depth is dialed to the meet, not measured.
- **The "cheater"** — a fine index/azimuth adjustment to offset a facet *between* teeth (compensate
  for lap variation, transfer error, or to level a row of meet points). Painting/digging are the
  diamond-industry analog of a deliberate cheater shift.
- **Diamond vs colored stone:** diamond brillianteering uses a **tang against a spinning scaife**
  (~3,000 rpm, diamond powder) and relies heavily on the cutter's **hand and eye**; colored-stone
  faceting uses a geared jig.

## 3. Correcting (improving) symmetry — and the weight cost

| Fault | Fix | Weight cost |
|---|---|---|
| Nicks / scratches (finish) | Re-polish | <1–2 pts; symmetry grade unchanged |
| Off-center table | Re-polish crown facets (table >2% off → unequal facets) | Low–moderate |
| Off-center culet | Even out pavilion facets (= equal pavilion angles) | Moderate |
| Crown/pavilion misalignment | Re-index / re-polish facets to realign meet points | Moderate |
| Out-of-round / wavy girdle | Full recut | **10–50%** depending on severity |

- **Re-cutting to upgrade a grade** usually doesn't pay — "the carat loss outweighs the improved
  performance for most people." Recut always reduces depth and/or diameter.
- **Planning software:** Sarine Advisor ("perfect symmetry" vs "asymmetric" options),
  OGI Smart Recut, DiamCalc Pro — compute the optimal re-polish and the geometric weight loss.

## 4. Deliberate manipulation — Painting & Digging

This is exactly the "keep the average ideal while shifting the small girdle facets" technique.

- The girdle is divided into **32 segments of 11.25°**. Manipulation = an **azimuth (index) shift**
  of the upper/lower girdle (halve) facets.
- **Painting** = halve facets shifted *toward* the mains (azimuth decreases; angle nears the main).
- **Digging out** = halve facets shifted *away* from the mains (azimuth increases).
- **Quantified:** **1 "click" = 3.75°; maximum 3 clicks = 11.25°.**

**Why:**
1. **Painting → weight retention** — less brillianteering loss → heavier stone (e.g. to hold 1.00 ct).
2. **Digging → clarity** — steepen upper girdles to polish away rough skin / naturals / inclusions
   near the girdle.

**Why it hides from averaged grades:**
> "Painting and digging out may not affect the symmetry grade, but it can affect the light
> performance — so a stone can earn Excellent symmetry yet miss AGS Ideal-0 light performance."

The main angles are **averages**, so shifting the small halve facets leaves the averages and the
symmetry grade looking fine while changing real light return. **GIA grades it in 4 tiers:**
Negligible / Moderate / Significant / Severe. Most labs don't comment on minor facets, so a
painted girdle can be **invisible on the certificate**.

**Performance fall-off (PriceScope analysis):** crown-only digging shows reduction at average crown
deviation **≥ 3°**; pavilion-only at average pavilion deviation **≥ 2°** (girdle facets degrade
fastest). *(Snippet-sourced; treat exact figures as approximate.)*

**Lab philosophy differs:** GIA penalizes painted upper girdles; AGS treats some **crown-only**
painting as *positive* for light return — but both agree **pavilion-side digging is always harmful**
and disqualifies Excellent / AGS-0.

**Detection:** ASET/Ideal-Scope — digging shows a **green semicircle of leakage** at the girdle;
severe painting shows a washed-out, low-contrast pattern.

## 5. Optical (Hearts & Arrows) symmetry — the highest-level tuning

- **Optical / 3D symmetry** = consistency of facet size, shape, angle and **azimuth** viewed 360°
  around the stone — distinct from GIA's **meet-point** symmetry. GIA does *not* grade azimuth/
  indexing/yaw.
- **How H&A forms:** Hearts (pavilion-side) = two lower-girdle facets + pavilion-main junction;
  Arrows (face-up) = reflections of the 8 pavilion mains.
- **Lower-girdle length is critical: ~75–78.5%** (too short → hearts touch; too long → clefts).
- **Tuning:** evenly index the 8 pavilion mains, equalize lower girdles, precise azimuth. Tolerances
  are extreme (cited: facet-size ~0.04%, culet-centering ~0.03%, girdle-facet angle ~0.03°).
- **Cost:** ~**3× polishing time** and ~**15% extra weight loss**; <1% of diamonds are cut this way.
- **Excellent symmetry ≠ Hearts & Arrows** (in both directions) — finishing grade is a different thing.
- **Verify tools:** H&A viewer (reveals yaw/indexing errors), Ideal-Scope (leakage), ASET (directional/
  edge asymmetry). GemConcepts demonstrates adjusting a single pavilion facet in **0.1° increments**
  and watching ASET color flip from red to blue — showing how fine the tweaks are.

## 6. Modern automation — making symmetry repeatable

- **Auto / laser bruting:** hand bruting leaves a barrel-shaped girdle; CNC + video-centering makes
  "the distance from center to each corner as equal as possible" (out-of-round control, ~5–20µm in
  vendor specs). **Synova DaVinci** lasers the girdle directly, making separate bruting redundant.
- **Scan-driven faceting:** OctoNus Helium / HP Carbon capture each facet's **inclination + azimuth**
  plus twist/MIC/roundness; Sarine Galaxy → Advisor → Strategist → Quazer laser chain.
  **Synova DaVinci** = first automated 5-axis laser full-faceting (up to 57 facets in one operation),
  leaving only final polish.
- **Repeatability:** one setup with automatic facet-to-facet transition → "improved stone symmetry,"
  enabling super-ideal / Hearts & Arrows at scale.
- **Closed loop:** patents (US 11,904,433; US 2022/0152772 A1) describe measure → compare-to-target →
  feedback → re-polish without human intervention. Sarine DiaMension HD periodically measures and
  DiaMark HD laser-marks corrections.
- **Lab-grown:** the symmetry benefit comes from the **cutting technology**, not the diamond's origin
  (more uniform rough can make automated plans more repeatable, but no inherent symmetry advantage is confirmed).

## 7. Bottom line — how symmetry is changed

1. **Mechanically:** set each facet's **angle (mast/protractor)** + **azimuth (index wheel, 3.75°/tooth)**
   and cut to **meet points**. 8-fold = every 12th tooth.
2. **Created:** bruting (round) → blocking (8-fold foundation) → brillianteering (refine).
3. **Corrected:** re-polish/re-index off-center table/culet, misalignment, wavy girdle — costing 1%–50% weight.
4. **Manipulated:** **painting/digging** — shift girdle facets' index up to ±11.25°, keeping main-angle
   averages ideal — for weight/clarity, at the cost of light return; often invisible on the certificate.
5. **Top-tier tuning:** optical Hearts & Arrows symmetry — 0.1°/0.03° tolerances, 3× time, 15% more weight.
6. **Today:** CNC/laser (e.g. Synova DaVinci) + scan-feedback loops make all of this repeatable at
   micron / sub-degree precision.
